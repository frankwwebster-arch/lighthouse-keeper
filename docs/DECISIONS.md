# Decisions made while building

- Standalone Vite + React + TypeScript app; engine is pure and seeded, so every behaviour is testable.
- No AI at runtime: typed commands go through a typo-tolerant matcher over a fixed command book. Close misses ask "did you mean?", nonsense gets a shrug.
- Quiz gating: only treats wait for an answered question; loo, food, wash and bed never do, so he cannot get stuck. Three wrong tries reveal the answer, which is typed in for no points.
- Questions appear after about 25 seconds idle and at least 150 seconds apart; times-table heavy.
- Chat questions follow the real clock (weekday afternoon asks about school, Saturday morning about football).
- Day is 07:00 to 21:30 game time, 8 real minutes. Bed from 19:00, annoyance from 20:00, forced to bed 21:30.
- Score out of 100 (mood 30, ship 20, friends 10, pet 10, house 10, brain 10, bedtime 10). Next day's allowance is 10 + 30% of the score. Up to 40 credits carry over.
- Camera: whole tower at rest; zooms to the object while used (not on the loo walk).
- Saves to the browser (localStorage) every 4 seconds and at day end.
- Production art drops in via the manifest, with vector fallbacks for undelivered assets. The first production batch supplies shell stripes and the three implemented room plates. Manifest entries may be a legacy filename string or `{ file, frames, fps }` for an animated horizontal strip.
- The first three gameplay floors use a fixed 110 × 35 logical core: kitchen, living room, bedroom with en suite. The toilet moved from the kitchen floor to the en suite. The hidden two-door changing zone belongs to the bedroom and reads its live height, so it follows the bedroom beneath the lamp room.
- Future standard-floor progression is deliberately non-linear. The lamp room stays topmost, the bedroom stays immediately beneath it, and the kitchen/entrance anchors the base. Each other unlocked standard floor uses the same 110 × 35 band, receives a one-time random middle-stack position, and keeps that saved position. Concept-art taper never dictates floor width, height or order; derive stripes from world Y.
- The bedroom's diving-board/changing-room extension moves upward with the bedroom and may provide a full-height dive into the sea. The reserved geometry now reads the bedroom's live height.
- The consolidated future-room, night-reveal, weather, transport, island-expansion, boiler, pool and solar direction is maintained in `docs/EXPANSION_DESIGN.md`.
- Basic bathroom access must stay convenient as the randomly ordered tower grows: integrate toilets/wash points into facilities such as the pool, gym and pub, add service WCs where needed, and route needs to the nearest available fixture rather than forcing repetitive full-tower walks.
- All clickable-object renderers accept `standard`, `on`, and `broken`. Broken visuals reuse integer-step casing wobble, smoke, and sparks. The seeded damage/repair loop is implemented; actual audio remains placeholder event hooks.
- Random keeper-asset breakdowns are controlled by grown-ups' frequency and maximum-concurrent dials (off by default). Large owned assets such as the garden/greenhouse, jetty, and future boat may fail. The shop belongs to somebody else and is never in the keeper's repair queue.
- The shop will unlock early as an off-island destination. The current on-island shop is a temporary playable fallback until boat travel replaces it. The preferred shoreline facility is a water-filled indoor boathouse/dry dock rather than a garage: launch directly towards the shop, refuel, repair and upgrade the rowing boat → tug → speedboat.
- A future workshop floor reduces breakdown pressure without eliminating faults; exact reduction and upgrade tiers remain to be tuned after playtesting.
- Production keeper parts use the same 32 × 40 canvas with explicit neck, shoulder and hip pivots. Rear-facing cooking and washing are deliberate reuse poses.
- Allowance is now driven by green need bars, not the overall score. Each of the 7 needs has a day-average; at or above the green line (default 50%) it is green. Tomorrow's allowance = guaranteed base + bonus x (greens / 7). One neglected need only costs one seventh of the bonus. The 100-point score stays as a fun rating with bonus marks.
- Grown-ups panel (Menu, then Grown-ups, PIN starts as 1234): dials for the base allowance, all-green bonus, green line, first-day credits, carry-over cap, price scale and per-food prices, plus gifting credits. Kept separately in the browser so new games keep the dials. Upgrade prices and upgrade gifting are in the same panel (see below).
- Players: each child has their own profile, save and dials. Quiz difficulty is a per-player dial (level 1 = age 7, 2 = about 9, 3 = 11+).
- Database: Neon Postgres via `@neondatabase/serverless`, optional. Tables (players, saves, player_rules, settings) are created on first use. No accounts: a player's id is an unguessable-ish slug; the grown-ups' PIN (hashed, server-side) protects the dials. Without `DATABASE_URL` the game falls back to localStorage. The production `/api/players` path has reported `db:true`; credentials still need the rotation noted in `docs/STATUS.md`.
- Mission dials follow the price pattern: one overall setting first (mission size %, reward), then optional per-goal and per-mission numbers that win. Progress is stored uncapped so raising a target loses nothing; lowering one below what he has done finishes the mission at once.
- Missions are all open at once and count in parallel, so floors arrive in whichever order he finishes them; a mission may wait for a taller tower (`needsFloors`). A finished floor is built overnight and appears the next morning (one a morning, queued in `arriving`); the lift goes in at once. The middle-stack order lives in the save (`middle`): a new floor takes a seeded random place once and keeps it. Old saves that had extra floors above the bedroom are re-stacked with the bedroom back on top.
- Upgrades are tiers on existing objects, bought from the object's own tap menu (no furniture shop). A tier's effect is the same for every object (more good, less time; numbers in `GAME.upgrades`); the bed's tier also sets the morning's energy at 80/90/100% as in the art brief, so tier 1 now wakes at 80 rather than the old 92. Buying a tier replaces a broken object. Prices follow the dial pattern: one overall % first, then a price per tier that wins. Grown-ups can gift the next tier for free.
- Every upgrade must provide a clear, measurable gameplay benefit, such as greater effect, shorter duration, higher capacity, better reliability, a useful new action or access to new content. A visual improvement may accompany that benefit but is never sufficient by itself. If an object has no worthwhile mechanical improvement, it remains single-tier rather than receiving a cosmetic-only upgrade.
- The upgrade list is a CSV (`data/upgrades.csv`), one row per object per tier, so Frank and Codex can work from a plain table. The game uses only object, tier, name and price; perks are design notes until coded. Frank edits in a claude.ai tracker page; Claude syncs page and CSV both ways on request (Frank owns names, prices, perks and notes; Codex owns the art-status columns).
