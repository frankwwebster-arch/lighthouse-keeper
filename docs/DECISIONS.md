# Decisions made while building

- Standalone Vite + React + TypeScript app; engine is pure and seeded, so every behaviour is testable.
- No AI at runtime: typed commands go through a typo-tolerant matcher over a fixed command book. Close misses ask "did you mean?", nonsense gets a shrug.
- Quiz gating: only treats wait for an answered question; loo, food, wash and bed never do, so he cannot get stuck. Three wrong tries reveal the answer, which is typed in for no points.
- Questions appear after about 25 seconds idle and at least 150 seconds apart; times-table heavy.
- Chat questions follow the real clock (weekday afternoon asks about school, Saturday morning about football).
- Day is 07:00 to 21:30 game time, 8 real minutes. Bed from 19:00, annoyance from 20:00, forced to bed 21:30.
- Score out of 100 (mood 30, ship 20, friends 10, pet 10, house 10, brain 10, bedtime 10). Next day's allowance is 10 + 30% of the score. Up to 40 credits carry over.
- Camera: whole tower at rest; zooms to the object while used (not on the loo walk).
- Saves to the browser (localStorage) every 4 seconds and at day end.
- Production art drops in via the manifest, with vector fallbacks for undelivered assets. The first production batch supplies shell stripes and the three implemented room plates. Manifest entries may be a legacy filename string or `{ file, frames, fps }` for an animated horizontal strip.
- The first three delivered gameplay floors use the historical 110 × 35 logical core: kitchen, living room, bedroom with en suite. Those assets remain immutable provenance rather than the target room geometry. The toilet moved from the kitchen floor to the en suite. The hidden two-door changing zone belongs to the bedroom and reads its live height, so it follows the bedroom beneath the lamp room.
- Future standard-floor progression is deliberately non-linear. The lamp room stays topmost, the bedroom stays immediately beneath it, and the kitchen/entrance anchors the base. Each other unlocked standard floor uses the common active standard-room width, receives a one-time random middle-stack position, and keeps that saved position. Concept-art taper never dictates floor width, height or order; derive stripes from world Y.
- The bedroom's diving-board/changing-room extension moves upward with the bedroom and may provide a full-height dive into the sea. The reserved geometry now reads the bedroom's live height.
- The consolidated future-room, night-reveal, weather, transport, island-expansion, boiler, pool and solar direction is maintained in `docs/EXPANSION_DESIGN.md`.
- Basic bathroom access must stay convenient as the randomly ordered tower grows: integrate toilets/wash points into facilities such as the pool, gym and pub, add service WCs where needed, and route needs to the nearest available fixture rather than forcing repetitive full-tower walks.
- All clickable-object renderers accept `standard`, `on`, and `broken`. Broken visuals reuse integer-step casing wobble, smoke, and sparks. The seeded damage/repair loop is implemented; actual audio remains placeholder event hooks.
- Random keeper-asset breakdowns are controlled by grown-ups' frequency and maximum-concurrent dials (off by default). Large owned assets such as the garden/greenhouse, jetty, and future boat may fail. The shop belongs to somebody else and is never in the keeper's repair queue.
- The shop will unlock early as an off-island destination. The current on-island shop is a temporary playable fallback until boat travel replaces it. The preferred shoreline facility is a water-filled indoor boathouse/dry dock rather than a garage: launch directly towards the shop, refuel, repair and upgrade the rowing boat → tug → speedboat.
- A future workshop floor reduces breakdown pressure without eliminating faults; exact reduction and upgrade tiers remain to be tuned after playtesting.
- Production keeper parts use the same 32 × 40 canvas with explicit neck, shoulder and hip pivots. Rear-facing cooking and washing are deliberate reuse poses.
- Allowance is now driven by green need bars, not the overall score. Each of the 7 needs has a day-average; at or above the green line (default 50%) it is green. Tomorrow's allowance = guaranteed base + bonus x (greens / 7). One neglected need only costs one seventh of the bonus. The 100-point score stays as a fun rating with bonus marks.
- Grown-ups panel (Menu, then Grown-ups, PIN starts as 1234): dials for the base allowance, all-green bonus, green line, first-day credits, carry-over cap, price scale and per-food prices, plus gifting credits. Kept separately in the browser so new games keep the dials. Upgrade prices and upgrade gifting are in the same panel (see below).
- Players: each child has their own profile, save and dials. Quiz difficulty is a per-player dial (level 1 = age 7, 2 = about 9, 3 = 11+).
- Database: Neon Postgres via `@neondatabase/serverless`, optional. Tables (players, saves, player_rules, settings) are created on first use. No accounts: a player's id is an unguessable-ish slug; the grown-ups' PIN (hashed, server-side) protects the dials. Without `DATABASE_URL` the game falls back to localStorage. The production `/api/players` path has reported `db:true`; credentials still need the rotation noted in `docs/STATUS.md`.
- Mission dials follow the price pattern: one overall setting first (mission size %, reward), then optional per-goal and per-mission numbers that win. Progress is stored uncapped so raising a target loses nothing; lowering one below what he has done finishes the mission at once.
- Missions are all open at once and count in parallel, so floors arrive in whichever order he finishes them; a mission may wait for a taller tower (`needsFloors`). A finished floor is built overnight and appears the next morning (one a morning, queued in `arriving`); the lift goes in at once. The middle-stack order lives in the save (`middle`): a new floor takes a seeded random place once and keeps it. Old saves that had extra floors above the bedroom are re-stacked with the bedroom back on top.
- Upgrades are tiers on existing objects, bought from the object's own tap menu (no furniture shop). A tier's effect is the same for every object (more good, less time; numbers in `GAME.upgrades`); the bed's tier also sets the morning's energy at 80/90/100% as in the art brief, so tier 1 now wakes at 80 rather than the old 92. Buying a tier replaces a broken object. Prices follow the dial pattern: one overall % first, then a price per tier that wins. Grown-ups can gift the next tier for free.
- Every upgrade must provide a clear, measurable gameplay benefit, such as greater effect, shorter duration, higher capacity, better reliability, a useful new action or access to new content. A visual improvement may accompany that benefit but is never sufficient by itself. If an object has no worthwhile mechanical improvement, it remains single-tier rather than receiving a cosmetic-only upgrade.
- The upgrade list is a CSV (`data/upgrades.csv`), one row per object per tier, so Frank and Codex can work from a plain table. The game uses only object, tier, name and price; perks are design notes until coded. Frank edits in a claude.ai tracker page; Claude syncs page and CSV both ways on request (Frank owns names, prices, perks and notes; Codex owns the art-status columns).
- Doors, stairway and costumes (agreed 9 Oct 2026; door lifecycle revised 16 Oct; full write-up in `docs/DOORS_STAIRS_AND_COSTUMES.md`): one continuous stairway separate from the rooms; every room, including the bathroom off the bedroom, has a door; the opaque door leaf hides him as he passes, and that is the only place he changes costume. Room doors swing both ways, always open away from the approaching Keeper, and close automatically only after he fully clears the doorway; they no longer remain open during the activity. The next activity decides the costume; he keeps what he has on until then. Teeth at bedtime means pyjamas on the way into the bathroom; in the morning he is back in normal clothes on the way to it. Already in the right room but in the wrong clothes: he steps out and back in, unless the room has its own changing place. Every standard floor is 56 px floorboards to ceiling; the stairway runs alongside the rooms; climbing goes ladders → spiral staircase → lift, with the slide outside the tower. He changes at both doors, so the stairway is always standard clothes. The en suite is reached only through the bedroom; there is a walk-in closet off the bedroom; pyjamas are used for washing and teeth from 19:00; naps happen in an armchair; the loo has its own cubicle; room doors never break and a broken front door never blocks travel.
- Pub/games-room dartboard placement (Frank, 17 Oct 2026): the approved Darts Context V8 board is attached to the room's right-hand cupboard-door leaf, not to a fixed wall. With the cupboard closed it is seen only at the established acute edge-on angle; opening that same door reveals the board fully face-to-camera. Preserve the board's practical Keeper reach height and treat the board as a child of the door transform. This is a future room-layout/object-layer rule only; no cupboard or room art is authorised by this decision.
- Animation speeds are a grown-ups' setting for the whole game, not a per-player dial (9 Oct 2026). Same 1–20 fps half-step range as Codex's review page. Frank writes each clip's proposed speed into its sprite sheet's metadata, which stays the real record. The in-game setting is a global tweak on top, kept for watching clips once they are wired to their activities; each tweak remembers the sheet speed it was made against, so when the metadata changes the sheet wins and the tweak drops away. Settled tweaks are copied out into the metadata. Animations now start from the moment they appear on screen, so a play-once clip shown mid-game runs in full.
- The recipe studio (`/studio`, 10 Oct 2026) is the design tool for how each activity plays: walks, doors and hidden costume changes, clips, timing and sounds. Claude and Codex draft recipes and macros as JSON in `data/studio/`; Frank fine-tunes by eye and approves. His edits are saved in the database and win over the drafts. Recipes are data, so new animations, objects and rooms mean new data rather than new code. Format: `docs/RECIPES.md`.
- Solid things (Frank, 10 Oct 2026): the keeper can never walk into a wall or a solid object, and nothing drawn in front of him (side-wall objects, an open door) may ever cover him while he is meant to be seen. Back-wall objects are drawn behind him and can be passed in front of. Only a hidden doorway step crosses a wall. The studio checks every visible frame against the frame's own opaque pixels. The exceptions are the object he is using and a door he is opening or closing.
- Sound effects are named placeholders until Frank supplies files: a placeholder plays a blip, so timing can be set now. Each cue has its own start, trim, volume and repeat. Sounds marked on an animation sheet (`sfxCues`) play in every recipe that uses that clip.
- Ambience and the backdrop are one build (10 Oct 2026): every moving outdoor layer has a sound partner, and the picture and the sound both read one weather value and fade together. Indoors is dry: rain, snow and fog are never drawn inside the cutaway rooms, and are heard muffled there. The great lamp never switches on instantly: it warms up and its lens turns up to speed over a couple of beats, with its own sounds, and its beam shows how polished it is. Instructions: `docs/AMBIENCE_AND_BACKDROP.md`.
- Every asset can be upgraded (Frank, 10 Oct 2026), so anything the keeper owns or uses is an object type with variants. One set of animations serves every variant: variants keep the keeper-facing contact heights and each declares where his feet go. Recipes name the type and its spot, the studio checks a recipe against every variant, and walks to an object stop just clear of it, so routes stretch when things move or change size.
- Night lighting (Frank, 10 Oct 2026): rooms are shaded rather than lit with light art. In the evening a room's light comes on as its door opens and goes off as it closes behind him, and the stairway is always lit. There are real sunrise and sunset sequences, not just a tint. Brief: `docs/BACKGROUND_ELEMENTS.md`.
- Doors and joins (Frank, 11 Oct 2026). He walks straight through an open door without stopping: behind the opaque open leaf he is out of sight for a moment, his costume changes there, and he walks out already changed, never popping into view. Every room keeps a clear area at each door where no object may stand. Walks finish on a whole stride and loops on a whole round, so clips join on the standing pose. The studio flags every join that jumps, and offers a look, a bridge or a note for Codex.
- Direct same-room locomotion (Frank, 14 Oct 2026): the Keeper must move naturally between two activity objects without leaving and re-entering the room merely to realign his gait. Every action returns to a registered standing or carry-ready anchor so the next walk can begin cleanly. The proposed common lattice is one 84 ku / 10.5 lp step, requiring a separately reviewed 42 ku start/stop root-motion candidate and a new opposite-foot F5 stop; approved image bytes and existing approval records remain frozen until those candidates pass visual review. Door occlusion remains the transfer boundary for entry, exit, costumes and inter-room travel, not the normal solution for object-to-object distance.
- Standard room target geometry (Frank, 14 Oct 2026): rebuild standard floors at 172 × 56 logical pixels. Standard floors share one active width; the inset lamp room, roof and declared local extension bays remain exceptions.
